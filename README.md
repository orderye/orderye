## The poem

> When spring doth loose her kites upon the air,  
> And summer holds its lotus to the blaze,  
> When autumn, true to date, doth loud declare  
> Red maples on my roof, in golden days,
>
> Then winter, with his leaden, weeping sky,  
> Doth hurl his snows like fury, wild and free;  
> The year hath never once betrayed the eye,  
> Nor missed an hour from its old decree.
>
> Yet I, in some far corner, weep and wail,  
> Though seasons, faithful, turn their measured wheel;  
> The world doth keep its faith, and will not fail,  
> But I am torn, and no solace can heal.
>
> So let the seasons keep their ancient art,  
> I weep, and winter weeps beside my heart.
